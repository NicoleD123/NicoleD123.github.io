Hello! My name is Nicole Deng. I am a research coordinator and lab manager at the SPAN Lab, Department of Psychology, Stanford University, working with Dr. Brian Knutson.

I study the neural mechanisms of affective experience: how affect is represented in the brain, how it interacts with cognition, and how these processes shape decisions and behavior. I use fMRI and quantitative methods to investigate these interconnected questions.

Recently, this has meant studying how affective responses relate to what people share on social media (co-advised by Dr. Jeanne Tsai), and whether neural responses measured in small samples can help predict individual choices and aggregate demand. I explore these questions in the contexts of vehicle purchases (supported by the Toyota Research Institute), movie demand, and fund investment decisions. You can read more about my ongoing work here.

Looking ahead, I hope to build on this work to further understand the neural mechanisms of affective experience. I am particularly excited about the interplay between affect and cognition. For example, I am interested in how cognitive processes regulate emotional responses and, conversely, how affect shapes cognition, including the organization of memory across event boundaries. I personally find these questions fascinating, as they move beyond traditional affective or cognitive science paradigms and bring those aspects together to explain how we experience, interpret, and remember the world. I am also increasingly drawn to methodological and fundamental questions about how affective information is represented within and across brain regions and which aspects of these representations are shared across people and contexts.

Before starting this full-time RA position, I earned B.A.s in Psychology and Neuroscience & Behavior at Wesleyan University, with a minor in Data Analysis, and wrote my capstone on how the brain organizes memory across event boundaries. Outside of research, I enjoy the arts (drawing, painting, photography, singing a cappella, and exploring museums and galleries) and all kinds of sports (tennis, top-rope climbing, strength training, snowboarding, and more).
