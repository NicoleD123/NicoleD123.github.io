Hello! I am a research coordinator and lab manager at the SPAN Lab, Department of Psychology, Stanford University, working with Dr. Brian Knutson.

My work centers on the neural mechanisms of affective experience: how affect is represented in the brain, how it interacts with cognition, and how those representations translate into decisions and behavior. I approach these questions using fMRI and quantitative methods, including parametric GLMs, multivariate decoding and neural signature development, inter-subject correlation (ISC), and out-of-sample forecasting.

Recently, this has meant studying how affective responses relate to what people share on social media (co-advised by Dr. Jeanne Tsai), and whether neural responses from small samples can anticipate choices and demand at larger scales (supported by the Toyota Research Institute). You can read more about my ongoing work here.

Looking ahead, I am interested in the intersection of affect and cognition, including how people regulate emotional responses and how affect shapes the organization of memory across event boundaries. I am also increasingly drawn to methodological questions about how we represent neural data, what information may be lost through common analytic choices, and how we can build models that generalize across people, tasks, and samples.

Before starting this full-time RA position, I earned B.A.s in Psychology and Neuroscience & Behavior at Wesleyan University, with a minor in Data Analysis, and wrote my capstone on how the brain organizes memory across event boundaries. Outside of research, I enjoy the arts (drawing, painting, photography, singing a cappella, and exploring museums and galleries) and all kinds of sports (tennis, top-rope climbing, strength training, snowboarding, and more).
